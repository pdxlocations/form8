# Automatic radio traffic and retries

Form8 keeps listening when there is no message to send. It still needs occasional radio traffic to discover stations, repair missed forms, and recover acknowledgments. **All LoRa packets use a shared broadcast channel**, including forms addressed to a callsign; the destination callsign does not turn the radio link into a private connection.

Idle broadcasts are deliberately slow. New/restarted stations trigger faster synchronization, and unanswered messages gradually back off. Random timing spreads stations out so they do not all transmit together.

## Background discovery and synchronization

| Why we transmit | When and how often | Repeats and limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Startup station/incident announcement — Hello** | Once when transmission first becomes allowed after startup. Contains callsign, active incident name/ID, and boot-session marker. **No periodic timer.** Incident joining, leaving, and radio reconfiguration do not create another announcement. | No receipt or automatic on-air repeat. A full local queue retries enqueueing after **2 seconds**. If startup transmission is disabled, that one startup announcement waits until transmission is allowed. |
| **New-node discovery response — Hello** | A valid packet arrives from a previously unheard origin, an origin last heard at least **90 minutes** ago, or a Hello shows a changed boot session. This includes nodes on other incidents. | Queue one announcement after **1–5 seconds** jitter, no earlier than **30 seconds** after our last queued announcement. Multiple arrivals combine into one pending response. Known nodes’ routine traffic does not trigger a response. Queue failures retry after **2 seconds**. |
| **Manual node ID request** | **Nodes → Request ID** sends one packet addressed to an unknown node ID, even across incidents or without joining one. Requires a working radio, saved callsign, and Enable radio transmission checked. | No automatic repeats or ACK. At most one request to each node per **30 seconds**, with up to 32 tracked targets. Identical unsent requests are combined. The addressed node queues one Hello if transmission is enabled and a callsign is configured; replies are limited to one per **30 seconds** total. A reply also satisfies any pending discovery announcement; there is no hourly timer. No companion app is required for an nRF reply. If the node is unreachable or has older firmware, no reply arrives. |
| **Manual incident announcement — Hello** | Click **Incidents → Announce incident** or **Announce** on the active saved incident. Requires an active incident and Enable radio transmission checked. | Queue one Hello immediately; identical unsent announcements are combined. There is no follow-up periodic announcement. It does not switch incidents, reset delivery retries, or start a local inventory pass. |
| **Position report — Position** | First fresh fix when the radio can transmit in an active incident; then every **10 minutes** by default. **Radio setup → Position sharing** changes this to **1–1440 minutes** or disables originating reports. Uses onboard GPS first, otherwise the connected phone. Fixes must be at most **2 minutes** old. | Up to **0–4,999 ms** random enqueue delay, plus channel delay. No ACKs or delivery retries. A full local queue retries after **2 seconds**. Latest unsent local report replaces its predecessor; queued reports expire after **2 minutes**, or sooner when fix age plus local queue time reaches that limit. Startup/join/configuration resets the timer. Other nodes relay reports once using ordinary TTL/duplicate suppression. Reports are excluded from queued/unread message counts. |
| **Quiet inventory page** | First checked **15 minutes** after startup/reconfiguration/joining, then one page every **15–18 minutes**. Each page lists up to **8 retained form bodies** and checksums. | No packet is sent if there are no bodies. Pages rotate through the active incident. At 200 bodies, a full quiet pass takes roughly **375–450 minutes**, before congestion. Queue failures retry the page after **2 seconds**. |
| **New/restarted-node synchronization — inventory pages** | A new/restarted station in our active incident, an incident change into our incident, or a same-incident station returning after the 90-minute offline threshold triggers a full inventory pass: first page after **5–15 seconds** (subject to cooldown), following pages every **30–45 seconds**. | At most one accelerated pass starts per **10 minutes**. Discoveries during an existing pass are combined rather than restarting it. A 200-body pass takes roughly **12–18 minutes**, before congestion. Empty inventories stay silent. |
| **Recovery of our saved manual ACKs** | On a form's normal, quiet inventory-page visit, re-advertise this station's saved manual ACK only if at least **30 minutes** have passed since its last tracked ACK queue attempt and its early repeats are finished. | This is a minimum interval per form, not a promise of one ACK every 30 minutes: large inventories take longer to revisit. Only retained bodies in the active incident participate. Reboot/reconfiguration clears the in-memory timing, so a later quiet page may repeat an older saved ACK. |

An inventory is also evidence that its publisher still holds a form body. A receiver can update that station's radio receipt from the inventory without sending an extra ACK packet just for that evidence.

## Traffic caused by sending, receiving, or repairing a message

| Why we transmit | Trigger | Retry behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **New form — Data** | You send a new message, reply, or correction. The saved form is queued immediately, subject to radio/channel availability. Long forms are split into packets. | Full-form retry waits after completed transmissions are **1 min, 5 min, then 15 min**. The 15-minute interval continues until one remote ACK arrives for a broadcast, or the named recipient ACKs a callsign-addressed form. There is a three-second receive-only window after each complete local form transmission. |
| **Manual resend — Data** | Click **Resend** on a stored form originated by this node, in its active incident, with transmission enabled. | Queue the same body/ID immediately subject to channel availability; coalesce if already queued. Existing receipts are preserved. This explicit transmission survives normal completed-delivery cancellation, including when the form was already ACKed, and is not suppressed as a repair response. Archiving/removing/leaving cancels queued work normally. Normal pending-form retry policy still applies if delivery has not been acknowledged. |
| **Chat text, reply, or reaction — Chat** | You click **Send chat**, add a reaction, or remove your own reaction. Text is limited to **200 UTF-8 bytes**; the JSON event including metadata is at most **768 bytes**, split into 160-byte fragments. | Initial send plus at most **two retries**: wait **15 seconds** after the first completed send, then **45 seconds** after the second. Any remote chat receipt stops retries. Reboot/incident changes stop pending attempts; radio reconfiguration in the same incident preserves the budget. A full body-job queue is retried after **2 seconds**. There is no chat history inventory or idle broadcast timer. |
| **Chat radio receipt — ChatReceipt** | A received chat event is durably saved, or the first fragment of an already-stored event with the same checksum is received again. | One receipt per trigger. Identical still-unsent receipts are combined. Sender retries may prompt another receipt; failed saves do not ACK. Chat receipts are separate from form and human acknowledgments. |
| **Missing-form request — Request** | An inventory advertises a body this radio does not have. Deliberately archived/discarded bodies are not requested again. | There is no separate periodic request timer. If the body is still missing when another inventory advertises it, a new request can be made. Identical requests still waiting in our queue are combined. Requests have the normal relay delay plus up to **2.8 seconds** of per-entry spreading within an eight-entry inventory. |
| **Repair body — Data** | A request matches a retained body and checksum on this radio. | Queue the matching body after a **1.5–4.5 second** response delay; an already-pending job for that body is reused. While waiting, hearing an alternative transfer postpones our response for **15 seconds** after its latest fragment. Hearing all fragments cancels our unsent response; an incomplete transfer resumes after silence. This also covers nRF companion body-fetch delays. Once our response has started, it finishes normally. No independent repair-response retry timer runs. Another request can trigger another response if needed. |
| **Radio receipt — Receipt** | A complete received body is successfully saved to flash; or the first fragment of an already-known body is received again. | One receipt per trigger, with identical still-unsent receipts combined. A sender's later Data retry can prompt another receipt. Inventories also provide receipt evidence. Failed saves do not generate a successful receipt. |
| **Operator acknowledgment — HumanAck** | You click **Acknowledge** and the acknowledgment is saved. Reading a form does not trigger this packet. | Initial queue attempt, then up to **two early repeats**, scheduled around **15 and 60 seconds** after the initial successful queue attempt. A full queue is retried after **2 seconds**. Slow recovery follows the background rule above. |
| **Manual ACK after a repair body — HumanAck** | This radio finishes sending a requested body that its own operator previously acknowledged. | Queue our saved manual ACK **after the final body fragment**, so the receiving station can store the body before processing the ACK. One per repair-body response; no separate retry timer for this response. |
| **Forwarding someone else's packet — relay** | A valid new packet arrives with forwarding allowance remaining and **Enable packet relaying** is on (off by default). Other-incident traffic is ignored except for Hello discovery packets and targeted ID requests. Position reports use the same relay rule. An ID request stops forwarding at its target. | Forward once after a random **100–1,299 ms** delay, plus queue/channel delays. Duplicate packets are suppressed by a **2-minute cache**. Packets allow up to **five forwards** after the originating transmission. A new retry/announcement is a new packet and can be relayed again. There is no idle relay timer. |

These intervals describe when work becomes eligible or is queued, not guaranteed on-air times. Higher-precedence forms, queued packets, reception already in progress, and a busy channel can delay transmission. Every eighth queue selection serves the oldest eligible item so routine/control traffic can still progress.

## Channel access, queue failures, and stopping retries

- After an ordinary packet transmission, allow a random **100–699 ms** gap. After a complete locally originated form or chat event, the **3-second receive-only window** takes precedence.
- If channel scanning finds activity or returns an error, wait **500–2,999 ms** before trying again. A channel scan is listening, not another broadcast.
- If starting a packet transmission fails, retry that frame after **2 seconds**. If a local Data frame cannot be requeued, unblock its form for a later full-form attempt instead of leaving it permanently marked queued. A dropped relay/control frame relies on the recovery rules above.
- Identical locally generated Hello, inventory, request, receipt, and manual-ACK packets that are still unsent are combined. The mesh keeps at most **48 queued frames** and **4 outgoing body jobs**; overflow is counted under Radio setup.
- An unchecked **Enable radio transmission** box, a missing/invalid callsign, or an unavailable radio prevents LoRa transmissions. The radio can still receive when its hardware is available. Enabling transmission/reconfiguring resets the in-memory schedules.
- A full-form retry stops after the first remote radio receipt for a broadcast, or the named recipient’s receipt for a callsign-addressed form. The sender’s own saved copy never counts as a remote ACK. Requested repair bodies are still sent in full for stations that missed them. Other tracked stations can remain Pending in the matrix after a broadcast’s overall Pending/outgoing indicator clears. Offline stations do not prolong a broadcast retry once another station has acknowledged. A human acknowledgment is not additionally required to stop a radio-delivery retry.
- Switching incidents does not announce the new selection; use **Announce incident** to share it immediately. Switching incidents clears queued work and retry schedules for the previous incident; its stored forms stay saved. Rebooting or reconfiguring the radio also restarts pending forms' retry sequence. Archiving/discarding cancels only that body's buffered transmissions and stale queued inventory pages; it does not restart unrelated messages or announce the unchanged incident again. A transmission already in progress may finish.

## Choosing which nodes relay

**Radio setup → Enable packet relaying** is off by default for new and previously unset configurations. An explicitly saved relay choice is preserved. Enable it on selected nodes that need to extend coverage; leave it off on nearby client nodes. The choice is saved on that radio. It stops forwarding other nodes’ packets, including discovery, ACK and position packets; the node can still originate messages, receive and acknowledge messages, and answer explicit requests for stored forms. It is not a receive-only switch. Keep relaying enabled on nodes needed to bridge a gap. An unchecked Enable radio transmission box blocks all outgoing traffic.

These slower schedules trade airtime for longer discovery and missed-message recovery. A new form still queues immediately; only subsequent retries wait longer. There is no airtime budget or suppression of ordinary duplicate relays yet.

## What happens on Wi-Fi instead of LoRa?

The phone/laptop webpage checks radio status every **2 seconds**. On connection, reconnection, a radio switch, or a detected reboot, it automatically sets the radio clock from the connected device. Failed time sync retries after **10 seconds**; ordinary polls do not repeatedly set time. Time sync is a local Wi-Fi HTTP request and does not transmit a LoRa packet. Phone GPS updates are local Wi-Fi requests at most once per minute while the client is visible and no fresh onboard fix exists; only the configured position broadcast timer originates LoRa position packets. Map tiles download over internet HTTPS and are stored locally; map downloading, export/import and map navigation do not send LoRa packets. Clicking **Refresh data**, using **Forget node** or **Hide offline nodes**, reading a message, editing a draft, updating the LCD, and measuring battery voltage do not themselves broadcast a LoRa packet. Sending a form, joining an incident, saving radio configuration, and manually acknowledging a form have the radio effects described above.

The Wi-Fi hotspot has its own discovery beacons, independent of LoRa: the pinned Arduino framework uses a **100-TU beacon interval**, approximately **102 ms**, while the hotspot is running. Wi-Fi association, connection maintenance, and webpage requests also generate Wi-Fi traffic. Those are not included in the LoRa TX/RX counters or the LoRa schedules above.

Update **all** participating radios to get the quieter behavior throughout the network. Older firmware can still originate and relay its more frequent control traffic. Slower quiet inventories trade background airtime for slower recovery of a loss that does not trigger a new-node pass or a pending-form retry; delivery still needs physical radio testing.

## nRF52840 with Android

The nRF build uses the same startup/manual/new-node Hello policy (**no periodic announcements**; discovery jitter 1–5 seconds and a 30-second cooldown), rotating inventory (**15–18 minutes**, eight forms/page), discovery repair pass (**30–45 seconds/page**, ten-minute cooldown; first page delayed 5–15 seconds), manual ACK repeats (**15 and 45 seconds**, then quiet-page recovery after at least **30 minutes**), five-forward hop allowance, two-minute duplicate cache, and default **10-minute** position report interval. Local queue failures retry after **2 seconds**. Relays wait **100–1,299 ms**; channel-busy delays are **500–2,999 ms**. Packet gaps are **100–699 ms**, with a **3-second** receive window after a completed local form/chat body. A transmit watchdog allows the calculated packet airtime plus **3 seconds** before requeueing a stuck frame.

Android polls its USB/Bluetooth status link every **2.5 seconds** in addition to the visible UI's ordinary **2-second** refresh. These are local-link requests, not LoRa broadcasts. The radio considers the companion present for **15 seconds** after its latest request. Inventories/repair serving require that companion to be present because the bodies live on the phone. Received bodies are ACKed after validated IndexedDB commit; up to three uncommitted bodies wait in RAM. A bounded **32-event** radio queue carries receipt, sent, and repair notifications; Android persists receipt metadata before removing the corresponding notification. If that queue fills, later notifications can be lost; existing sender retry/inventory recovery remains available.

The companion schedules form retries after final-transmission events at **60, 300, then 900 seconds**, and **900 seconds** thereafter until one remote station ACKs a broadcast, or the named recipient ACKs a callsign-addressed form. Chat gets an initial attempt and at most two retries, **15 then 45 seconds** after completion, and stops on any remote chat ACK. A missing completion notification or interrupted link lets the companion requeue a body after a **120-second** local watchdog; existing jobs coalesce by ID. Reconnection clears local in-flight tracking and resumes eligible durable records. These timers require the companion to run; they are not background Android service guarantees.

Without Android, a configured nRF radio can continue relaying (if enabled) and sending Hellos/onboard positions. It does not durably receive new bodies, advertise/serve the phone's stored bodies, or run full-body retries. Bluetooth discovery uses connectable Nordic UART advertising at a fast **20 ms** interval, then **152.5 ms** after the core's fast-advertising timeout; USB polls are not RF traffic.

## ESP32 Bluetooth client traffic

ESP32 advertises the Form8 Nordic UART service at **100–150 ms** advertising intervals (BLE controller timing/jitter applies). The Wi-Fi hotspot remains available during discovery and pairing; authenticated Bluetooth connection pauses Wi-Fi. Bluetooth disconnect restores the same hotspot. Idle authenticated links expire after **30 seconds** without a request; unfinished pairing expires after **60 seconds**. The app's ordinary **2-second** status refresh keeps an active connection alive. BLE notifications are paced at least **5 ms** apart, sized to negotiated MTU (up to **244 bytes**) and retried locally if the BLE host cannot queue the notification. Input is limited to **16 KiB**, with a bounded **64-fragment** receive queue; overflow disconnects the client. These are local 2.4 GHz client-link activities, not LoRa broadcasts.

All ESP32 forms, chat, radio receipts, manual ACKs, repair and retry schedules use the same storage/API for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Merely scanning, pairing, reading, polling, disconnecting, or restoring the hotspot does not originate a LoRa packet. Sending, joining, configuring, and manually acknowledging still have their ordinary radio effects. Pairing protects the Bluetooth client link, not LoRa traffic.

## Delete an incident and its stored traffic

In **Incidents**, use **Delete active incident & messages…** or **Delete incident & messages…** beside a saved incident. The confirmation identifies the local radio/companion store and explains that all stored form bodies, form metadata/receipts, linked forms, chat messages, chat replies, and reactions for that incident will be deleted. Deleting the active incident also leaves it and clears its queued traffic. Other incidents, drafts, archived device copies, and other nodes’ copies are kept. The result reports deleted form and chat-event counts. Deletion does not transmit a LoRa announcement or remote-delete command.

Without periodic Hellos, a powered but silent node can be shown Offline after 90 minutes. This is the existing last-heard rule; use **Request ID** or a manual announcement to check it. Inventory, message retries, relaying, and configured position broadcasts keep their separate schedules.
